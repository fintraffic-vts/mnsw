EMSWe RIM Sender Integration – Access Point Setup Guide

How to connect your own AS4 Access Point to the Finnish MNSW RIM
Setup Guide | PMode Configuration Guide

Who this is for. Declarants and Data Service Providers who want to submit EMSWe formalities to the Finnish Maritime National Single Window (MNSW) through the Reporting Interface Module (RIM) over eDelivery AS4.

This page gives the Finland-specific values you need to get started. The authoritative EU-level specification is EMSA's RIM v3.3.0 Integration Guidance. Finland's detailed requirements are in the Finland EMSWe Domibus Sender Integration Guide (PDF).

Connectivity is established step by step. You first connect to our DEV RIM. Once the AS4 message exchange works there, end-to-end testing continues in Staging (STG), and only after that in production.

You send us

  • Party ID and AS4 endpoint URL
  • Public AS4 certificate

2. Architecture and responsibilities

EMSWe RIM messaging follows the eDelivery four-corner model. You own and operate corners C1 and C2. MNSW operates C3 and C4.

Senders do not use RIM as their Access Point. According to EMSA's guidance, RIM is the Member State's receiving component. Senders need their own eDelivery AS4 conformant Access Point. Any conformant solution can be used, for example Domibus with the default WS plugin, which is the setup used in chapter 6 of EMSA's guidance.
Source: EMSA RIM v3.3.0 Integration Guidance, chapter 6.

A Data Service Provider (DSP) may operate C2 on behalf of one or more declarants. This is a commercial arrangement between the sender and the DSP. MNSW does not provide a shared Access Point.

4. Access and URAM registration

Access to the platform is always granted at the organisation level. Individual user accounts are not used, so you do not need separate access for each developer or contact person.

MNSW registers your organisation in the Finnish URAM sub-domain on your behalf. In testing, the registration uses the shared test eIDAS certificate described in section 5.2. To complete the registration, send us:

  • Organisation name
  • EORI number
  • Address and country
  • Contact person(s): name, email and phone number
The identifier must match exactly. RIM validates every incoming message against URAM. The value you send in Authorization.Identifier must be exactly the identifier registered in URAM, otherwise the message is rejected.

5. Certificates and security

Three different certificates are involved, and they have different purposes.

5.1 AS4 certificate exchange

  1. Add the MNSW DEV public certificate (mnsw-dev.mnsw.fi.cer, delivered by MNSW) to your Access Point truststore. If it was delivered with a .txt extension for email transport, remove the extension first.
  2. Send us your public AS4 certificate so that we can add it to our truststore.

Encryption is mandatory. As specified in EMSA's guidance, RSA-based signing and encryption is used, with the security policy eDeliveryAS4Policy_BST_PKIP_RIM and profile rsa. MNSW uses Domibus 5.2.1.

5.2 eIDAS certificate

For testing, EMSA has provided Finland with a test eIDAS certificate that MNSW distributes to its test senders as a keystore (FI1059876543.zip). The keystore alias is FI1059876543; the passphrase is delivered together with the keystore.

Testing only. The test certificate is shared among all test participants. Never use it in production. For production, the sending organisation chooses between a qualified and an advanced eIDAS certificate.

EMSA's samples use the Belgian test certificate and EORI BE01234567895. When you use them, replace those with the Finnish test certificate FI1059876543 and your own values.

7. Building the AS4 UserMessage

Creating the signed ASiC-E container is the responsibility of the declarant's backend (C1); the sender-side Access Point (C2), whether operated by the declarant or a DSP, creates the AS4 message. EMSA's guidance contains sample messages; chapter 6 has a complete AS4 UserMessage example using the Domibus WS plugin.

7.1 Three levels of metadata

The UserMessage carries metadata on three levels. Keeping them apart avoids the most common configuration errors.

    subgraph UM["AS4 UserMessage"]
        L1["Level 1: PartyInfo

        L2["Level 2: MessageProperties

originalSender = your EORI / finalRecipient = FI"]
        L3["Level 3: PartProperties of cid:message

7.3 Example UserMessage (Domibus WS plugin)

This illustrative example is based on chapter 6 of EMSA's guidance, adapted to the Finnish DEV environment. Values in {curly brackets} are placeholders to be replaced with your own values.

submitRequest – emswe-formality-request
<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
<soap:Envelope xmlns:soap="http://www.w3.org/2003/05/soap-envelope"
               xmlns:ns="http://docs.oasis-open.org/ebxml-msg/ebms/v3.0/ns/core/200704/"
               xmlns:eu="http://eu.domibus.wsplugin/">
  <soap:Header>
    <ns:Messaging>
      <ns:UserMessage mpc="http://docs.oasis-open.org/ebxml-msg/ebms/v3.0/ns/core/200704/defaultMPC">
        <ns:MessageInfo>
          <ns:Timestamp>{yyyy-MM-ddTHH:mm:ss.SSSZ}</ns:Timestamp>
          <!-- Must be unique for every message (duplicate detection is enabled) -->
          <ns:MessageId>{UNIQUE_MESSAGE_ID}</ns:MessageId>
        </ns:MessageInfo>

        <!-- Level 1: PartyInfo = access points (C2 -> C3) -->
        <ns:PartyInfo>
          <ns:From>
            <ns:PartyId type="urn:oasis:names:tc:ebcore:partyid-type:unregistered">{SENDER_PARTY_ID}</ns:PartyId>
            <ns:Role>http://docs.oasis-open.org/ebxml-msg/ebms/v3.0/ns/core/200704/initiator</ns:Role>
          </ns:From>
          <ns:To>
            <ns:PartyId type="urn:oasis:names:tc:ebcore:partyid-type:unregistered">mnsw-dev.mnsw.fi</ns:PartyId>
            <ns:Role>http://docs.oasis-open.org/ebxml-msg/ebms/v3.0/ns/core/200704/responder</ns:Role>
          </ns:To>
        </ns:PartyInfo>

        <ns:CollaborationInfo>
          <ns:Service type="rim-process-schema">rim-messaging-service</ns:Service>
          <ns:Action>emswe-formality-request</ns:Action>
          <ns:ConversationId>{CONVERSATION_ID}</ns:ConversationId>
        </ns:CollaborationInfo>

        <!-- Level 2: MessageProperties = business sender (C1) and recipient (C4) -->
        <ns:MessageProperties>
          <ns:Property name="originalSender" type="emswe-sender-eori">{SENDER_EORI}</ns:Property>
          <ns:Property name="finalRecipient" type="emswe-ms-rim">FI</ns:Property>
        </ns:MessageProperties>

        <!-- Level 3: PartProperties = RIM metadata and URAM authentication -->
        <ns:PayloadInfo>
          <ns:PartInfo href="cid:message">
            <ns:PartProperties>
              <ns:Property name="Authorization.Identifier">{IDENTIFIER_REGISTERED_IN_URAM}</ns:Property>
              <ns:Property name="Authorization.SubDomain">FI</ns:Property>
              <ns:Property name="Authorization.Type">{DECL_OR_OTHER}</ns:Property>
              <ns:Property name="FormalityInfo.Formality.TypeUrn">{FORMALITY_TYPE_URN}</ns:Property>
              <ns:Property name="FormalityInfo.Formality.FileName">{FORMALITY_FILE_NAME}.xml</ns:Property>
              <ns:Property name="FormalityInfo.FormalityVersion">{FORMALITY_VERSION}</ns:Property>
              <ns:Property name="FormalityInfo.CorrelationId">{CORRELATION_ID}</ns:Property>
              <ns:Property name="FormalityInfo.CreationDateAndTime">{yyyy-MM-ddTHH:mm:ss.SSSZ}</ns:Property>
              <!-- Only if the container includes attachments -->
              <ns:Property name="FormalityInfo.AttachmentCount">{N}</ns:Property>
              <ns:Property name="FormalityInfo.FormalityBody.Attachment1">{ATTACHMENT_1_FILE_NAME}</ns:Property>
              <ns:Property name="MimeType">application/vnd.etsi.asic-e+zip</ns:Property>
              <ns:Property name="FormalityInfo.MimeType">application/vnd.etsi.asic-e+zip</ns:Property>
            </ns:PartProperties>
          </ns:PartInfo>
        </ns:PayloadInfo>
      </ns:UserMessage>
    </ns:Messaging>
  </soap:Header>
  <soap:Body>
    <eu:submitRequest>
      <!-- Signed ASiC-E container, sent as an MTOM attachment -->
      <payload payloadId="cid:message" contentType="application/vnd.etsi.asic-e+zip">
        <value>cid:{ASIC_E_CONTAINER_FILE_NAME}</value>
      </payload>
    </eu:submitRequest>
  </soap:Body>
</soap:Envelope>

The FormalityInfo.AttachmentCount and FormalityInfo.FormalityBody.Attachment* properties are only included when the ASiC-E container has attachments. Keep MimeType, FormalityInfo.MimeType, and the WS-plugin payload contentType aligned as application/vnd.etsi.asic-e+zip so Domibus resolves the message to the intended PMode leg.

8. Message flow and responses

Both directions use One-Way / Push. MNSW pushes its responses to your AS4 endpoint, so your Access Point must also act as a receiver.

sequenceDiagram
    participant C1 as C1 Sender backend
    participant C2 as C2 Your Access Point
    participant C3 as C3 MNSW RIM
    participant C4 as C4 MNSW backend
    C1->>C2: Signed ASiC-E + metadata
    C2->>C3: AS4 UserMessage (emswe-formality-request)
    C3-->>C2: AS4 Receipt (synchronous, in HTTP response)
    C3->>C4: Validated formality
    C4->>C3: Result
    C3->>C2: AS4 UserMessage (emswe-formality-response), HTTPS push
    C2-->>C3: AS4 Receipt
    C2->>C1: Response delivered to backend
Your endpoint must be reachable from MNSW over HTTPS. Send us the source IP addresses your Access Point sends from and its DNS name, so that firewall openings can be arranged on both sides.

10. Moving to production and changes

Before production, replace the shared test eIDAS certificate with your organisation's own qualified or advanced eIDAS certificate, and switch to the production party details delivered by MNSW.

Any later change to your Access Point (certificate, endpoint, key or IP addresses) requires advance notification, certificate exchange, a PMode update, testing in STG and a coordinated production activation.
